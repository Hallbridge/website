Websites

What we found on 37,000 contractor websites.

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

In November 2026 we scanned the websites of 37,316 U.S. construction companies with 5 to 50 employees, across general contractors and every major trade. The goal was simple: see what an owner or general contractor finds when they look a company up.

1 in 15 sites is gone

For 2,477 companies, about 6.6 percent, the domain no longer pointed anywhere or showed a parked page. Another group returned errors or did not respond to our scan. If a buyer clicks through from a directory listing and finds nothing, the conversation usually ends there.

A third still show an old year

Of the sites that show a copyright year in the footer, 34 percent still show 2025 or earlier, and about 1 in 5 show 2023 or earlier. It is a small detail, but it is one of the first things a careful buyer notices, and it makes a company look less active than it probably is.

4 in 10 have no projects page

We could not find a projects, portfolio or gallery page on 39 percent of the sites. For a contractor, past work is the strongest proof there is. Without it, a buyer has to take your word for it or call someone else.

More than a third hide their email

On 37 percent of sites we could not find an email address anywhere on the pages we checked. Many rely on a contact form alone, which means a buyer has no way to reach a person directly. Of the sites that did list an email, 6 percent used a free Gmail, Yahoo or similar address instead of the company domain.

What most sites do get right

The good news: about 95 percent of the sites are at least set up for phones, and more than half run on WordPress, so most of these problems are content fixes, not rebuilds. Updated projects, a current footer, a real email address and a working bid button go a long way.
