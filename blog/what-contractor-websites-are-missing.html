What most contractor websites are missing.

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

As recruiters, we look at a lot of construction companies the way candidates and clients do: online, first. So we looked at a large number of U.S. contractor websites, from general contractors to every major trade, to see what an owner, a general contractor or a job candidate actually finds.

Some sites are simply gone

A meaningful share of companies had a website that no longer worked or showed a parked page. If a buyer clicks through from a directory and finds nothing, the conversation usually ends there.

Many look older than the company is

Roughly a third of the sites that show a year in the footer were out of date, some by several years. It is a small detail, but it makes a busy company look quiet.

About 4 in 10 show no projects

Many sites had no projects or portfolio page at all. For a contractor, past work is the strongest proof there is. Without it, a buyer has to take your word for it or call someone else.

Many make it hard to reach a person

A large share of sites had no email address anywhere, relying on a contact form alone. Others used a personal Gmail or Yahoo address instead of the company name.

What an updated online presence can do

  • Win more work. Owners and general contractors check you out before they invite you to bid. Recent projects and a clear list of what you do get you on more lists.
  • Back up every proposal. When your site matches the quality of your work, your proposals and referrals land better.
  • Hire better people. Candidates research employers too. A careers page with real photos of your team makes the offer easier to accept.
  • Show up in search. A clean, current site with accurate details helps the right people find you on Google and in AI search.

The good news is that most of these fixes are straightforward, and the payoff shows up in every bid, every hire and every referral.
