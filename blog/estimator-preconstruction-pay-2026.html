Estimator and preconstruction pay in 2026.

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

Preconstruction is where builders win or lose work before a shovel hits the ground, and the pay reflects it. We looked at 442 estimating and preconstruction postings with a stated range, almost all from 2026.

The ladder

RoleTypical rangeMedian
Estimator$101K – $130K$112K
Senior Estimator$122K – $165K$141K
Preconstruction Manager$140K – $180K$156K
Chief Estimator$140K – $190K$167K
VP of Preconstruction$162K – $207K$185K

Base salary only. Typical range is the 25th to 75th percentile of posted midpoints.

The biggest single jump is from estimator to senior estimator, about $29,000 at the median. That step usually comes with owning bids end to end and building the relationships with subs that make numbers reliable.

By region

Texas and Oklahoma posted the highest estimator median at $125,000, followed by the West Coast at $120,000. The Southeast was lowest at $105,000. For senior estimators the West Coast led at $162,000, well ahead of the Midwest, Mountain West and Texas, which all sat in the mid $130s.

Preconstruction managers posted a $160,000 median in Texas and Oklahoma, $150,000 in the Southeast and Mountain West, and $140,000 in the Midwest.

What it means if you're hiring

  • A good senior estimator is often the hardest seat to fill. Price the role off the senior band, not the estimator band, if you need someone who can run a bid alone.
  • Preconstruction managers and chief estimators overlap in pay. Be clear which one you need: one leads the process with owners and designers, the other leads the numbers.
  • Candidates compare more than base. Bid-day expectations, travel and how much of the team they inherit all weigh in.
