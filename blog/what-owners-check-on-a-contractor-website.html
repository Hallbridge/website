What owners and GCs check on a contractor's website.

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

Before an owner, developer or general contractor sends a bid package, someone on their team looks you up. Many of them are on a phone, and they do not spend long. They are trying to answer three questions.

1. Do you do the work I need?

Your services should be obvious on the first screen: the trades you self-perform, the building types you know and the area you cover. A list of every service you have ever done is harder to read than a clear statement of what you do best.

2. Have you done it well before?

This is where most contractor sites fall short. In the hundreds of contractor websites we have reviewed this year, the most common gaps were project photos that had not been updated in years, project names buried in images that search engines can't read, and footers still showing a copyright year from a decade ago. Each one quietly tells a buyer the company may not be busy.

Show recent, named projects with good photos, the type of work, and the general contractor or owner where you can. A few strong projects beat a long gallery of small thumbnails.

3. How do I reach you?

Make it easy. A phone number and email that work on a phone, a contact form that actually reaches someone, and a clear button for bid invitations. We regularly find request buttons that link to the wrong email address, dial a placeholder number or go nowhere at all.

A quick self-check

  • Open your site on your phone. Can you read it without zooming?
  • Is your newest project on the site from this year or last?
  • Does every button and email link on the contact page reach a real person?
  • Does the footer show the current year?

If any of those come up short, the fix is usually smaller than people expect.
