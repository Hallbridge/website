Superintendents vs. project managers: who gets paid more in 2026?

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

Ask around a jobsite and you will hear both answers. We looked at what builders actually publish: 866 project manager postings and 652 superintendent postings with a stated pay range, almost all from 2026.

Nationally, it's nearly a tie

The median posted base for a project manager is $132,000. For a superintendent it is $130,000. The middle of the market for both roles runs from about $115,000 to the high $140s.

At the senior level the field side pulls slightly ahead: $160,000 for a senior superintendent against $158,000 for a senior project manager. General superintendents post a median of $174,000.

By metro, superintendents often come out ahead

Of the 30 metros where we had at least 10 postings for each role, superintendents posted a higher median than project managers in 15. The gap was widest in Austin ($152,000 vs. $130,000), the San Francisco Bay Area ($167,000 vs. $154,000) and San Diego ($150,000 vs. $138,000).

The highest posted superintendent medians were in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, New York and New Jersey, Austin and San Diego. For project managers the top markets were New York and New Jersey, Los Angeles, the Bay Area, Orange County and Phoenix.

What it means if you're hiring

  • Don't assume your PM band works for your superintendents. In many markets the field side now costs as much or more.
  • Posted ranges run conservative. Strong superintendents with a clean record on similar work are often hired at or above the top of the band.
  • Base is only part of it. Truck or car allowance, per diem and bonus often decide which offer a superintendent takes.
