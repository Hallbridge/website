Superintendents vs. project managers: who gets paid more in 2026?

By Alex Hall · November 3, 2026

Ask around a jobsite and you will hear both answers. Based on what general contractors and developer-builders are offering this year, the honest answer is: it depends on the market.

Nationally, it is close to a tie

A typical project manager and a typical superintendent both land at around $130,000 in base salary, with most offers falling somewhere between the mid $110s and $150,000.

RoleTypical base
Assistant Project Managerabout $95K
Project Managerabout $130K
Senior Project Managerabout $160K
Assistant Superintendentabout $105K
Superintendentabout $130K
Senior Superintendentabout $160K
General Superintendent$175K and up

Rounded base salary. Bonus, vehicle and per diem are not included.

In many markets, the field side leads

In roughly half of the major metros, superintendents are now offered more than project managers. The gap is widest in fast-growing markets like Austin and across California, where experienced field leaders are hardest to find.

Pay gets them to look. Your presence gets them to call.

Strong candidates rarely take a call from a company they know nothing about. Before they reply to a recruiter or apply, most of them look you up. A current website with recent projects, real photos of your crews and a careers page that explains what it is like to work for you does a lot of the selling before the first conversation. When two offers are close, the company that looks established and busy usually wins.

What it means if you are hiring

  • Do not assume your project manager pay band works for superintendents.
  • Expect strong candidates to land at or above the top of the posted range.
  • Make sure what they find online backs up the offer: recent work, real people and a clear picture of the company.
